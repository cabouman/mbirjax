Flash remediation

Fixing the FoV-truncation "flash" in mbirjax MBIR reconstruction · started 2026-07-08 · plan of record: flash_remediation_plan.md (this directory)

When a scanned object extends outside the reconstruction support — wider than the field of view, or taller than the covered slab — MBIR must explain measured attenuation with voxels that cannot represent its source. The unexplained attenuation piles up at the support boundary as a bright flash (a radial ring, or bright end slices with axial ringing), degrades the interior, and drags the convergence stop metric. This project characterizes the mechanism on controlled synthetic cases and settles, per axis, which remediation to use: sinogram weight tapering (the split_sino_recon precedent) versus reconstruction-support padding (scale_recon_shape). The pages below are self-contained (figures embedded) and read in order.

Phase 1 — What is the flash, and what causes it?

The two truncation cases at a glance (with schematics), the enlarged-ground-truth reproduction method, and the characterization results: the radial ring plus whole-interior bias, the one-sided axial flash with z-ringing, and the padding mechanism check that confirmed both are model-support problems. Complete.

Phase 2a — The axial case, settled

The geometry that frames axial truncation (visibility bound: z-padding is always cheap), the seven-variant taper-vs-padding experiment, and three robustness regimes (wide cone, sharp regularization, photon noise). Verdict: pad in z by the geometry-derived scale, no taper — the row taper is retired for this case. Complete.

Phase 2b — The radial case, settled

The radial counterpart: no lateral visibility bound (only the physical rotation clearance bounds the object), so the knee is empirical. Padding-scale sweeps across four overshoots and four regimes land one answer: pad to cover the object — no more, no less — the knee is regime-robust, over-cover mildly hurts, and the channel taper is falsified (it cannot reach the interior bias). Complete.

Phase 2c — The split_sino_recon seam: the stripes, their cause, and the fix

On real data (Lilly D01788) the no-taper split produces structural ±40% seam stripes. Reproduced synthetically and root-caused: the load-bearing driver is a sub-row misalignment between the sinogram cut row and the recon split slice (~0.4 rows on Lilly; default synthetic models lock the grids and can never show it), acting on the extension-end truncation. The shipped sine taper suppresses the stripes at 4× downsampling but fails at 8×; the geometry-derived deeper extension fixes both regimes, and exact cut–split alignment removes the driver at zero cost. Proposals (plan only): hrecon from geometry and/or align the split with the cut. Complete.

Phase 2d — Recommended remedies

The synthesis: per case (axial, lateral, split seam), the proposed fix with rationale, equations, implementation sketches, and an honest pros/cons weighing. Verdicts: axial — extend to the exact geometric bound (1 + R/SID, R from the recon grid) automatically in auto_set_recon_geometry (a holder always leaves the FoV at one end, so truncation is the norm); lateral — detect and warn, deliberately no auto-padding (cover is not derivable and under-padding is catastrophic); split seam — geometry-derived hrecon as the default, grid-shift alignment opt-in, taper retired with it. The design record; implemented 2026-07-11/12. Complete.

Phase 3 — The implemented remedies on real scans

All four Phase 2d verdicts shipped as defaults and validated step by step on SiC, Lilly, and the Purdue BGA scan: the axial extension removes the SiC end flash and ringing, reconstructs the real object continuation, and reaches the 0.2% stop at ~iteration 20 vs ~49; the split extension meets or beats every seam yardstick with the taper retired; the lateral warning is correct on all four scans and corrected two of our own labels (z62 is genuinely contained — its ring is not truncation flash; Lilly is a mild true positive), with its advice verified on BGA (lateral 1.5× erases the boundary ring and reveals the board past the old FoV); the NSI cleanup recovers ~14% of the Lilly volume with values preserved. Complete.

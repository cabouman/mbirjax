Flash remediation — Phase 2: which remediation actually works?

Living document — one section per sub-campaign, updated as they run · started 2026-07-08 · plan of record: plans/flash_remediation/flash_remediation_plan.md · Phase 1 characterization: phase_1_results.html · scripts: plans/experiments/flash_remediation/

TL;DR so far (P2a done; P2b and P2c pending). For axial (row) truncation, the answer is: pad the recon in z by a geometry-derived scale, and do not taper. Padding beats the row taper at every level (0.141 vs 0.344 truncated-end NRMSE), adding a taper on top of padding makes things worse, and it turns out z-padding is bounded by pure geometry — no measured ray reaches |z| > (SID+R)/SID × the half-slab, confirmed bit-exactly — so "full" padding is always affordable. The row taper is retired for the axial case.

1  Where Phase 2 stands

Phase 1 established that both flash artifacts are model-support problems and that scale_recon_shape padding essentially eliminates them. Phase 2 asks the follow-up question: which remediation should actually ship, per axis, and with what parameters? Three sub-campaigns, run in the order Greg picked:

  • P2a — axial: row taper vs z-padding vs combinations. Done — §2.
  • P2b — radial: the padding-scale knee, plus a channel-taper cell kept as a falsification control. Pending — §3.
  • P2c — the split_sino_recon seam: current taper design vs truncate vs extend-without-taper. Pending — §4.

The machinery carries over from Phase 1: an enlarged-truth-grid phantom projected through the real detector, iteration-by-iteration tracking, error split by region against the aligned truth crop, and visual quality as the deciding metric. One bookkeeping note: the laminate phase in the P2a phantom shifted relative to Phase 1 (the band pattern is computed on the larger truth grid), so don't compare P2a's absolute numbers to Phase 1's — every cell within P2a shares one phantom, which is the comparison that matters.

2  P2a — axial truncation: taper vs padding

The setup is the Phase 1 SiC-like case: a laminated cylinder (radius 0.75× the FoV, so no lateral truncation) whose top extends past the covered slab, cone beam at magnification 2, 128 views, 40 iterations. On it we ran a seven-cell grid: padding at four levels {none, partial ×1.094, full ×1.188, overfull ×1.7} crossed with the row taper {off, on}, the taper being the quarter-sine ramp from the split_sino_recon precedent applied to the truncated side's edge rows. Taper widths come from the geometry — 6 rows on the default slab, 3 on the partially padded slab, and 0 on the fully padded slab (so the "full + taper" cell forces 6 rows, deliberately, to see what tapering costs when geometry says it isn't needed).

Before the grid ran, the geometry handed us a structural result. With the source in the iso plane (a circular orbit — the setting of all these cases), a ray to the top detector row climbs highest at the far edge of the FoV, reaching |z| = hmax·(SID+R)/SDD — that's (SID+R)/SID times the half-slab, just 1.125 here. Material beyond that height never projects onto the detector at all. We confirmed it the blunt way: extending the phantom from 1.6 to 4.0 half-slabs changed the sinogram by max |Δ| = exactly 0.0, bit for bit. That kills the planned "object too long to pad" scenario — it cannot exist in z. Full padding is always cheap (scale 1 + R/SID plus a small PSF margin, 1.19 here), the right scale is computable rather than swept, and padding past the bound should buy nothing — which the grid then verified: overfull ties full to the third decimal.

What this settles

All three hypotheses got clean answers. Taper-alone blurs real end structure (H1): confirmed — it trades the ringing for the prior-extrapolation smear and stays 2.4× behind any padding. With full padding the taper is unnecessary (H2): confirmed, and then some — it isn't just unnecessary, it actively degrades the end (0.141 → 0.220). The combo wins at partial padding (H3): refuted — the taper hurts there too (0.154 → 0.215). The reason H3 fails is the geometry bound again: the visible-material band above the slab is thin (at most 12.5% of the half-slab here), so even "partial" padding covers most of it, leaving the taper nothing to fix and data to destroy.

One honest ceiling runs through all of it: every variant, full padding included, smooths the final laminate band in the top ~3 slices. Voxels at the top of the slab project past the detector edge for roughly half the views — the classic cone-beam end wedge — so those slices are half-sampled. That is missing data, not inconsistency; no weighting or padding can restore it, and it sets the quality floor any remediation should be judged against.

The P2a recommendation: for axial truncation, pad in z by the geometry-derived scale — (max_visible_z + PSF margin) / half-slab, available as cone_axial_geometry() in the experiment machinery — and do not taper. The row taper is retired for the axial case; the niche it was designed for (padding impractical) does not exist in z. For the split-seam study this tilts the prior toward the no-taper variant, but the split is a genuinely different situation (its extension gets data through overlap rows, and the prior-context requirement is central there), so P2c still gets its own measurement.

3  P2b — radial padding knee (pending)

Planned: a padding-scale sweep on the lateral-truncation case to find the quality-vs-memory knee. Unlike z, radial padding is not geometry-bounded — outside material at any radius projects into the sinogram — so the knee is empirical, and lateral padding costs ~scale² in memory. A channel-taper-only cell rides along as the falsification control (the mechanism says it cannot remove the interior bias), plus a pad + channel-taper combo at partial padding.

4  P2c — split_sino_recon seam A/B (pending)

Planned: the current seam design (sino overlap + sine taper + recon overlap) vs truncate-at-the-split vs extend-without-taper vs geometry-tuned widths, on a phantom with laminate structure crossing the split, each stitched result judged against an unsplit full recon. P2a sharpens the hypotheses but doesn't decide them — at the split, the extension's data arrives through overlap rows and its role as qGGMRF prior context is the heart of the question.

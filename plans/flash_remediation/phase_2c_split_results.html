Flash remediation — Phase 2c: the split_sino_recon seam

Which split design produces the cleanest seam: the shipped sino-overlap + sine-taper, no taper, a deeper recon overlap, or truncating at the split? · started 2026-07-08 · overview and other pages: index.html · the axial and radial cases: phase_2a_axial_results.html, phase_2b_radial_results.html · plan of record: flash_remediation_plan.md · scripts: plans/experiments/flash_remediation/

TL;DR. split_sino_recon halves the memory of a cone-beam reconstruction by reconstructing the detector-row halves separately and stitching. The A/B on its seam design lands cleanly: the sinogram overlap is load-bearing, the sine taper is not. Cutting the sinogram at the split costs a visible dark seam band (NRMSE ~0.18, six times the reference's own truth error) — on smooth objects as much as structured ones. With the overlap kept at full weight, the split reproduces the unsplit reconstruction to the float-noise floor (2–3×10−5); the shipped taper moves it away by a harmless but unnecessary ~10−3. Proposed change: drop the taper, keep the overlaps.

1  Why the seam is subtle

Two facts frame the design, worked out in the plan's mechanism discussion. First, the data term is almost separable at the split: with a circular orbit the source sits in the iso plane, so a ray to a detector row above iso never crosses below the iso plane — and a voxel above the plane never projects onto rows below iso. The only cross-split coupling in the data is local blur (the voxel footprint, the fractional iso-row rounding, pitch mismatch): a few rows and slices, independent of the cone angle. On the data term alone, cutting cleanly at the split would cost almost nothing.

Second, the qGGMRF prior is not separable: neighboring slices couple, and over iterations that influence propagates several slices deep. Each half's recon therefore extends past the split, and those extended slices act as prior boundary conditions for the kept slices — what matters is their accuracy, because the kept boundary slices are smoothed toward them. That is the real job of the sinogram overlap: not to inform the kept slices (separability says it barely does), but to make the extended slices data-accurate. And the sine taper reads as a graded data-to-prior transition across the extension, rather than a hard boundary where the data ends — the kind of hard constraint edge Phase 1 showed rings. The variants below pull these three design elements apart.

The split design — side view

The three design elements under test: each half keeps the detector rows on its side of the iso row plus a few overlap rows beyond it (orange bracket), reconstructs its slices plus a few overlap slices past the split (dashed bands), and the shipped design multiplies the overlap rows' weights by a quarter-sine ramp falling to zero at the extreme row. The stitched result blends a few slices around the split and discards the rest of the extensions.

2  Experiment design

The phantom is fully inside the FoV and the covered slab (a 0.75×-FoV cylinder, z-extent ±0.85 of the half-slab), so there is no physical truncation and every seam artifact is attributable to the split design. Two versions: a structured phantom with laminate layers crossing the split and a sphere straddling it (the hard case — the prior-extrapolation failure mode needs structure at the seam to show itself), and a smooth control (no laminate). Each variant's stitched result is judged against an unsplit reference reconstruction at the same iteration count, with a seam-region NRMSE (interior disk × split ±4 slices) and z-profiles across the split. The variants:

  • current — the shipped design: sino overlap + sine taper + recon overlap (h = 5 rows/slices).
  • no_taper — identical geometry, taper off: isolates what the taper itself contributes.
  • no_taper_deep — taper off, recon overlap deepened so every extended row's contributions are fully representable (hrecon = ⌈hsino·(1+R/SID)·pitch⌉ + PSF margin): separates the taper question from the extension-depth question.
  • truncate — the sinogram cut exactly at the iso row, recon overlap kept: the prior-extrapolation variant, predicted to tie on the smooth control and lose where structure crosses the split.

The split logic is a local, parameterized reimplementation of split_sino_recon's geometry (the library is untouched); regularization comes from the full sinogram and is copied to the halves, exactly as the library does.

What this settles

The sinogram overlap is load-bearing; the sine taper is not. The verdicts, in order of decisiveness:

  • Never truncate the sinogram at the split. Cutting at the iso row costs a seam NRMSE of ~0.18 — six times the reference's own error against ground truth — as a visible dark band, on smooth objects as much as structured ones. The overlap's real job is exactly what the mechanism analysis said: making the extended slices data-accurate so they serve as correct prior context (and as sane material for the stitch blend).
  • With the overlap at full weight, the split is essentially free. The no-taper variants reproduce the unsplit reconstruction to a seam NRMSE of 2–3 ×10−5 — the scale of GPU run-to-run nondeterminism — on both phantoms. The data-term separability at the iso plane is not approximate in practice; it is numerically exact once the extensions are fed.
  • The sine taper is unnecessary — and measurably, if harmlessly, counterproductive. Tapering the overlap rows moves the result away from the unsplit reference by ~10−3 (invisible in any image, ~30× the no-taper deviation). The taper made sense as insurance against inconsistent overlap data; but the overlap data is not inconsistent — every extended row's contribution is representable by the extended slices — so down-weighting it just perturbs the objective. This mirrors P2a's lesson at the physical detector edge: tapers subtract information wherever the model can already explain the data.
  • The default extension depth is already sufficient: deepening the recon overlap (no_taper_deep) changes nothing at the 10−5 level, so the shipped h = 5 covers the actual coupling width (PSF + rounding) with room to spare at this geometry.

Proposed library change: remove the sine taper from split_sino_recon (keep the sino and recon overlaps exactly as they are). The gain is small — the tapered seam error is already invisible — but the change is a strict simplification that makes the split agree with the unsplit reconstruction to numerical noise, and it retires the last taper in the codebase for consistency with the Phase 2 verdicts. One caveat carries over from the mechanism analysis: all of this rests on the circular orbit (source in the iso plane); if the zero-helical-shift restriction is ever relaxed, the separability breaks and the design needs re-testing.

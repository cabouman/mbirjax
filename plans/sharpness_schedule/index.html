The sharpness/snr_db schedule study

Voxel-cylinder streaks under aggressive regularization: reproduction, mechanism, and remediation · started 2026-07-25 · plan of record: sharpness_schedule_plan.md and phase_b_plan.md (this directory)

Large sharpness / snr_db settings produce fine vertical streaks — voxel-cylinder-organized error, coherent along z — in MBIR reconstructions of laterally truncated scans, strongest when the VCD iteration starts at coarse subsets. This project reproduced the artifact on a real BGA scan and a matched synthetic testbed, built and validated a spectral severity metric for it, evaluated the per-granularity regularization schedule as a remedy, and traced the artifact to its mechanism. The pages below are self-contained (figures embedded) and read in order.

Phase A — The artifact, the instruments, and where it forms

Reproduction on the real scan (severity 7.65× its control at the first iteration, persisting at full resolution at 5× the downsampled level); the two-seed difference and axial-spectrum P(fz) metrics with their validation; the subset-footprint attribution (first-updated subsets carry ~40% extra streak energy); and the synthetic testbed — a null at first, then hardened to reproduce the artifact by adding lateral truncation (the switch, ×20) and a larger cone angle (×13 more once truncation is present). Complete.

Phase B — The per-granularity schedule, evaluated: negative

The pre-registered test of conservative-early regularization schedules (data-side, prior-side, and joint variants at two depths). All six variants add streak energy (+4–22% at the gate iteration, peaks +11–57%) at essentially zero convergence cost, with a clean mechanism reading: prior-side schedules hurt most, the signature of saturation during formation. No winner under the pre-registered rules; no full-resolution confirmation run. Complete.

The converged reconstruction — the streaks are in the solution

The mechanism story: a two-start experiment (production FDK start vs the ground truth itself, optimally scaled) shows both converge to the same streaked solution while the objective descends cleanly — on the truncated testbed the artifact is the minimizer's own content, not convergence drift. The error field is an interior version of the truncation flash: +32% of the truth's mass deposited inside the domain, and a sharpness sweep shows the total is conserved while the prior sets its expression scale — severity tracks the fine-scale fraction. Lateral padding removes the artifact entirely on the testbed (156×) but only modestly on the real scan (−23%: more unmodeled-mass sources than lateral truncation); the real scan run long converges into the artifact, with direct numbers for the iteration-count decision. Complete.

The second family — beam hardening, its instrument, and its testbed

Padding unmasked a second, ray-aligned artifact family on the real scan. Its instrument: residual transfer curves in the MAR metal coordinate (the real padded scan shows the concave metal-ray dip; unpadded shows none). A two-metal polychromatic testbed with a severity dial reproduces the family with hardening as the only pathology — object-anchored (rotated-view control, correlation 0.982), mass-removing (−17% at full severity, the signed mirror of truncation's +32%), and invisible to the vertical-family metric (orthogonal rulers). Ends with the calibrated case (s ≈ 0.2, dense lattice) beside the real scan and the MAR bench + demo this enables. Complete.

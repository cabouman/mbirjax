Does dropping the first full-volume iteration change the reconstruction?

Overview and summary

MBIRJAX's default VCD schedule D0 = [0,2,4,6,7] begins with a granularity-0 iteration: a single subset containing every voxel cylinder, i.e. a full-volume update. That first iteration is the memory high-water mark of the whole reconstruction, and we would like to drop it. The candidate schedule D1 = [2,4,6,7] simply starts at granularity 2 (4 subsets) instead. This page shows head-to-head reconstructions — same data, same regularization, same iteration count; the only difference is the schedule.

Bottom line: on three real datasets at two sharpness settings, D0 and D1 are visually indistinguishable at 15 and 20 iterations, and the quantitative metrics agree (D1 ties or very slightly leads D0 in every measured cell). The signed difference images contain no coherent structure inside the object — only boundary speckle and faint bands at the axial ends and at the cone fan plane, all at a few percent of the display window. Dropping the granularity-0 iteration is safe.

Data and methods

  • Datasets. lilly_ds8 and lilly_ds4: the NSI D01788 autoinjector scan preprocessed at downsample (8,8) with view subsample 8 (sinogram 225×235×187, recon 187×187×286) and at (4,4)/4 (sinogram 450×470×374, recon 374×374×667). z62: the ORNL Versa Z62 cylinder (partition-study cache, 201 views, recon 512×512×640).
  • Reconstruction. Cone-beam MBIR with transmission_root weights; sharpness ∈ {1.0, 2.0} set before auto_set_regularization_params (auto-regularization then frozen). Both schedules run the plain library solver with the same seed, so subset partitions match. Volumes were captured at 15, 20, and 30 iterations in one continuous run per arm.
  • Reference. Where shown, the leftmost column is a 150-iteration default-schedule reconstruction of the same cost function — effectively the converged answer both schedules are heading toward. (lilly_ds4 at sharpness 2.0 has no cached reference; that comparison is image-only.)
  • Metric. The numbers above each figure group are cropped log10 NRMSE vs the reference — log10 of the relative RMS difference over the interior (disk of 0.85× the recon radius, 10% of slices excluded at each axial end). More negative = closer to converged; −1.0 means 10% RMS from the reference, −1.3 ≈ 5%.
  • Volumes. All captured volumes and references are in depot at /depot/bouman/data/mbirjax_metrics/slice_parity/ (recons/, refs/) for inspection with slice_viewer.

What to look for

Each figure shows an axial mid-slice (top row) and a coronal mid-cut (bottom row). Columns: reference (when available) | D0 | D1 | signed difference D1 − D0. The grayscale window is shared across the recon columns; the difference panel has its own symmetric limit, printed in its title — check that limit against the window to judge how small the differences are (they peak at a few percent of the window everywhere).

  • Interior structure: the decision-relevant question. Any coherent structural difference between D0 and D1 inside the object would argue against dropping the iteration. None appears in any panel.
  • Boundary speckle in the difference images (e.g. rings at the syringe barrel): the two runs' subset-noise realizations differing at high-contrast edges, not a structural change.
  • Bands at the axial ends (top/bottom of the coronal cuts): the cone-beam flash zones, excluded from the metric and addressed separately by the flash-remediation work.
  • A band at mid-height on lilly_ds4 sharpness 2.0: the cone fan plane (center slice). See the zoom section at the bottom — both schedules have the same noise spike there; the difference image highlights it only because the two runs' noise realizations diverge most where voxels are least anchored.

Center-slice (fan-plane) zoom — lilly_ds4 sharpness 2.0

The difference band at mid-height in the lilly_ds4 s2.0 coronal cut sits at z ≈ 329–331: the cone fan plane (the volume midpoint is 333; the offset matches the detector row offset). The rightmost panel below plots per-slice axial second-difference RMS — the "slice decoupled from its neighbors" noise signature — for both schedules. Both show the same sharp spike at the fan plane, and the two curves essentially coincide: D1 is higher by less than 1% at 15 iterations and less than 0.1% at 30, against a ±20% slice-to-slice variation of the same metric. The center-slice effect is a property of the surrogate and the cone geometry, not of the schedule; the difference image highlights that band only because the least-anchored voxels are where two otherwise-equivalent runs diverge most.

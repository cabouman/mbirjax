MBIRJAX experiment reports

Internal findings pages for the MBIRJAX cone-beam CT reconstruction project — convergence, image-quality, and reconstruction-artifact studies on real scan data. Each entry is a self-contained report.

Center-slice noise

Why a few slices at the volume center go noisy in cone-beam MBIR and converge slowly: the coherent source-plane mode family, its ~70× gain mountain, the joint-step overshoot that pumps it, and the remedy the campaign converged on — rank-one mode-selective damping with a drive-based decide-once trigger, validated in-sample and on a held-out scan. Includes the dead-end map (refuted hypotheses and measurement-artifact cautionary tales) and the full experiment index (cs1–cs26).

Single self-contained report; the implementation spec and decision record live in the repo (plans/bugs_and_artifacts/center slice noise/).

Flash remediation

Diagnosis and remedy of the bright "flash" artifacts that field-of-view truncation produces in cone-beam reconstructions. A multi-phase study: characterizing the axial and radial truncation cases, the split_sino_recon seam, the recommended support-padding remedies, and their validation on real scans. Has its own overview page linking to each phase.

Sub-pages: Phase 1 (characterization), Phase 2a–2d (axial / radial / split-seam / remedies), Phase 3 (implemented remedies on real scans).

Voxel-cylinder streaks under aggressive regularization

Why large sharpness/snr_db settings produce fine vertical streaks in laterally truncated cone-beam scans, and what actually fixes them. The arc: reproducing and instrumenting the artifact on a real BGA scan and a matched synthetic testbed; a pre-registered per-granularity regularization schedule evaluation (negative — every variant made the streaks worse); the mechanism — the streaks are the converged solution's own content, a deposit of the truncation's unexplained attenuation whose spatial expression scale tracks the prior's strength; lateral padding as the remedy that removes the late-iteration growth; and the second, ray-aligned artifact family (beam hardening), instrumented by residual transfer curves and reproduced with a calibrated polychromatic testbed.

Sub-pages: Phase A (the artifact and the instruments), Phase B (the schedule evaluation), the converged-solution mechanism study, and the beam-hardening study; the area's own index links them in reading order.

Slice-parity alternation

Does splitting each update into even-slice and odd-slice phases (red–black Gauss–Seidel in z) improve cone-beam convergence by decoupling the axial data term that drives the center-slice artifact? Covers the mechanism, the parallel-beam null control, the state-dependence (parity helps only after a coarse start), and the error-reduction trend across three datasets. Conclusion: a useful optional quality knob at high sharpness, not a default-schedule change.

Skip-0 schedule comparison

Does dropping the first full-volume (granularity-0) iteration — the memory high-water mark of a reconstruction — change the result? Head-to-head default vs skip-0 reconstructions on three datasets at two sharpness settings, with per-slice difference images. Conclusion: visually indistinguishable and metrically equivalent, so the iteration can be dropped for its memory savings.

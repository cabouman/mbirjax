MBIRJAX experiment reports

Internal findings pages for the MBIRJAX cone-beam CT reconstruction project — convergence, image-quality, and reconstruction-artifact studies on real scan data. Each entry is a self-contained report.

Flash remediation

Diagnosis and remedy of the bright "flash" artifacts that field-of-view truncation produces in cone-beam reconstructions. A multi-phase study: characterizing the axial and radial truncation cases, the split_sino_recon seam, the recommended support-padding remedies, and their validation on real scans. Has its own overview page linking to each phase.

Sub-pages: Phase 1 (characterization), Phase 2a–2d (axial / radial / split-seam / remedies), Phase 3 (implemented remedies on real scans).

Slice-parity alternation

Does splitting each update into even-slice and odd-slice phases (red–black Gauss–Seidel in z) improve cone-beam convergence by decoupling the axial data term that drives the center-slice artifact? Covers the mechanism, the parallel-beam null control, the state-dependence (parity helps only after a coarse start), and the error-reduction trend across three datasets. Conclusion: a useful optional quality knob at high sharpness, not a default-schedule change.

Skip-0 schedule comparison

Does dropping the first full-volume (granularity-0) iteration — the memory high-water mark of a reconstruction — change the result? Head-to-head default vs skip-0 reconstructions on three datasets at two sharpness settings, with per-slice difference images. Conclusion: visually indistinguishable and metrically equivalent, so the iteration can be dropped for its memory savings.

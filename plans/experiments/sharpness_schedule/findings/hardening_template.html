The second family: beam hardening, its instrument, and its testbed

The sharpness/snr_db schedule study, beam-hardening arc (2026-07-26), branch sharpness_schedule. Background: converged_streaks.html (the deposit mechanism and the padding results this page follows from) · scripts: plans/experiments/sharpness_schedule/{real_bga, mechanism, driver}/ · demo: demo/demo_10_mar.py.

TL;DR

Lateral padding removed the truncation streaks from the real scan and unmasked a second artifact family: near-horizontal, ray-aligned bands around the ball grid. The working hypothesis — beam hardening — is now instrumented, tested, and reproduced on the synthetic testbed.

  • The instrument is the residual transfer curve: bin the converged run's residual sinogram by the MAR metal coordinate (segment the reconstruction, project the metal). On the real padded scan the residual dips on metal-heavy rays with the hardening sign; the unpadded scan shows no metal organization (truncation swamps it); the plastic-coordinate control is flat.
  • A two-material polychromatic testbed reproduces the family: with hardening as the only pathology, the reconstruction develops the deficit ("cupping"), interball dark bands, and z-localized face bands — and a rotated-view-set control (error correlation 0.982) proves the structure is object-anchored, not view aliasing.
  • The deposit framework generalizes with sign: truncation deposited +32% of true mass; hardening at full severity removes −17%. Unmodeled mass of either sign is deposited at the expression scale the prior allows.
  • The two families are instrumentally orthogonal: the vertical-family severity Slow stays at its floor across every hardened case — each family needs, and now has, its own ruler.

Product: a severity dial calibrated against the real scan (the real padded residual dip sits near s ≈ 0.2 in this parametrization), a ground-truth-equipped bench for the metal-artifact-reduction work on the release goals list, and a user-facing demo (demo_10_mar.py).

1. The question the padding results left open

When the real scan is reconstructed with lateral padding, the truncation family — the fine vertical, z-coherent striping this study began with — largely disappears, and what remains visible is different in kind: bands roughly aligned with the source-to-detector rays, stamped at the heights of the ball rows, plus fine texture at the package faces. In the (x, z) view cone rays are near-horizontal lines, so per-ray data errors backproject as near-horizontal bands: the geometry of the residual family itself says the error is being generated per ray, in the data domain. The natural cause for per-ray error on metal-heavy paths is beam hardening — this scan is uncorrected (bga_no_hart) — but "looks ray-aligned" is not an attribution. Two things were needed: an instrument that measures ray-association without assuming the answer, and a testbed where hardening can be switched on alone.

2. The instrument: residual transfer curves in the metal coordinate

If the error is generated per ray, measure it where that statement is native: the sinogram domain. At (near-)convergence the residual e = y − Âx is exactly what the linear model refused to explain. For each ray we also have a physical cause coordinate: the metal thickness it traverses — obtained exactly as the library's MAR machinery does (mbirjax.preprocess: Otsu segmentation of the reconstruction, forward projection of the metal class), so the curve lives in the same coordinate the MAR beam-hardening model fits. The instrument is then just: bin the residual by the metal coordinate and plot the mean. Genuine beam hardening must produce a concave dip — metal-heavy rays measuring less attenuation than the linear model expects — while the plastic coordinate serves as a control. Nothing about ball positions, rows, or orientations enters the ruler.

How to read it: each point is the mean residual over rays binned by the metal coordinate m (error bars: per-bin standard deviation — the full residual spread, mostly noise and unrelated structure; the mean is the systematic part). Left, padded: the mean dips on the metal-heaviest rays (−0.014 in the top bin against +0.005 mid-range) — the hardening sign — and the plastic-coordinate control (not shown) is flat. Right, unpadded: no metal organization at all; the truncation deficit dominates the residual everywhere (rms 0.113 vs 0.060 padded). The hardening fingerprint only becomes measurable once padding removes the larger unmodeled-mass source.

What the magnitude means — and does not mean. The residual is only the undeposited part of the hardening deficit: a converged run with a weak prior absorbs most of the per-ray deficit into the image — that absorption is the artifact. So the −0.014 dip is a lower bound on the injected nonlinearity, not its size, and severity calibration must use the residual and the image together (§5). The synthetic sweep makes this concrete: the residual dip is non-monotone in true severity, because stronger hardening deposits a larger fraction.

3. The testbed: a parametric polychromatic forward model

The simulator (driver spectrum.py + the materials mode of the ball-grid phantom) aims for the correct structure of the nonlinearity with a single severity dial, not spectroscopic fidelity:

  • Materials. The phantom decomposes exactly into a plastic slab plus per-metal ball maps — multiple metal types with different hardening factors, assigned across the lattice in a checkerboard, with different reference-energy values (6.0 and 4.5 here) so the metals are also separable by intensity segmentation.
  • Physics. A filtered-bremsstrahlung spectrum (Kramers' law × aluminum filtration, 24 energy bins) and per-material energy dependence in the standard two-basis form — photoelectric ((E/Eref)−3) plus Klein–Nishina Compton — with one hardening factor wpe per material (slab 0.15; metals 0.8 and 0.55).
  • The severity dial. s ∈ [0, 1] interpolates each material's ratio curve toward flat, with one refinement found in unit testing: each curve is normalized so its spectrum-weighted mean is exactly 1. The linear model is then the tangent at zero path length at every severity — the dial bends pure curvature without recalibrating the small-path slope, the deficit is nonnegative, and it is monotone in s. At s = 0 the model is bit-for-bit the existing monochromatic pipeline (asserted in every run before any reconstruction).

4. Results: hardening alone produces the family, object-anchored

The attribution panels

How to read it: error against the reference-energy ground truth at iteration 16, axial ball-layer slice (top) and (x, z) mid-plane (bottom), window ±0.006 (≈ 0.6× the slab value). Left: no hardening — isotropic speckle. Middle: hardening as the only pathology — the slab region develops the slab-wide negative deficit (cupping) with darkening concentrated between and around the balls, and z-localized bands at the slab faces in (x, z). Right: the truncated + padded configuration with its denser ball grid — grid-aligned interball dark bands in the axial plane and face bands with fine vertical needle texture in (x, z): the character of the real padded scan's residual family.

Three checks pin the attribution:

  1. Object-anchored, not aliasing. Re-running the full-severity case with the view angles rotated by half an angular step changes the error field almost not at all (correlation 0.982 at the ball layer). Metal-pair hardening structure is fixed by the object; view-sampling artifacts would have rotated with the trajectory.
  2. The signed ledger. Deposited mass runs 0.000 → −0.079 → −0.170 across severities 0 → 0.5 → 1 (contained case): hardening removes mass, the mirror image of truncation's +32% deposit. One framework covers both: unmodeled mass of either sign is deposited at the expression scale the prior allows.
  3. Instrumental orthogonality. The vertical-family severity Slow stays at the contained-null floor (6×10−6 → 1.3×10−5) in every hardened case — measured confirmation that the two families need separate rulers, and that the Phase A instrument was never scoring this family.

The MAR coordinates check out against truth. On the synthetic, transfer curves binned by the true metal path sinograms and by the MAR-segmented coordinates agree within ~3% for the dominant metal (with class labels ordered by intensity, and some mixing on the weaker metal) — the instrument used on real data measures what it claims to.

5. The calibrated case

The severity sweep brackets the real padded scan's residual dip between s = 0 and s = 0.5, landing near s ≈ 0.2. The calibrated run uses that severity with a denser ball lattice (smaller pitch and radius — many more ball pairs, closer to the real package), in both the contained and the truncated-padded configurations.

6. Where this leaves things

  • The real scan's error budget is now decomposed into two attributed families. Truncation → the vertical, z-coherent deposit (positive mass; removed by padding); hardening → the ray-aligned interball family (negative mass; untouched by padding). Each has a validated instrument: the two-seed / P(fz) pair for the first, the residual transfer curve for the second.
  • The MAR work on the release goals list now has a bench: ground truth, a severity dial calibrated to the real scan, multiple metals with distinct hardening factors, and the transfer curve as the acceptance instrument — a correction should flatten it. The natural first experiment is recon_plastic_metal on the calibrated case, scored before/after.
  • A user-facing demo ships alongside: demo/demo_10_mar.py — polychromatic ball-grid simulation with user-set data / hardening / reconstruction / MAR parameters at the top of the file, standard vs MAR reconstruction, results in the slice viewer.

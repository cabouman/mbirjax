The streaks are in the solution: the converged reconstruction under truncation

The sharpness/snr_db schedule study, mechanism follow-up (2026-07-26), branch sharpness_schedule. Background: findings.html (the artifact and the instruments, Phase A) and phase_b_results.html (the schedule evaluation, Phase B) · scripts: plans/experiments/sharpness_schedule/mechanism/.

TL;DR

After the schedule search came back negative, the question turned to the artifact's late-iteration behavior: on the hardened synthetic testbed, the streak severity grows through the tail of the run. Is that slow, wandering convergence — or is the optimizer heading exactly where the objective points? A two-start experiment settles it: a run started from the ground truth itself converges to the same streaked solution as the production run, while the objective descends cleanly in both. The streaks are not a convergence artifact here; they are the minimizer's own content.

  • Both starts merge onto one growing severity curve (within 8% by iteration 5, identical from iteration 20), and their final volumes agree to 1.6% max / 2% rms — the convex objective's single limit point, reached from both sides.
  • Started at the truth, the optimizer walks away from it on purpose: error against the ground truth rises 0.39 → 0.57 while the data residual falls 0.208 → 0.089 — the streaked solution fits the truncated data 2.3× better than the truth does.
  • The seed-dependent part of the artifact is a transient — it decays two decades over the long run — but at the practical 15-iteration budget it is still far from gone.
  • Where the error lives supports a simple mechanism: the reconstruction deposits +32% of the ground truth's mass inside the domain — the out-of-view attenuation it must explain somehow — roughly half packed onto the boundary ring (the familiar flash) and half spread through the interior as fine-scale, z-organized texture: an interior version of the flash.

Consequence: for the persistent (seed-independent) streak field, update-side remedies cannot help — the fix must change the objective or the model. A same-day follow-up wave (§6) tested the mechanism and the leading remedy: the deposit's total is conserved across a 16× severity sweep while its expression scale tracks the prior's strength; lateral padding removes the artifact entirely on the testbed (156×), and on the real scan removes the flash ring and the entire late-iteration growth (×10.5 → flat) while the seed-dependent transient decays faster — though at the 15-iteration budget the transient itself drops only 23–25%. The streaks also form with no cone at all (parallel beam, truncated: 134× the contained level) — the mechanism is geometry-general, with the cone acting mainly as the transient's amplifier. Direct numbers for the iteration-count decision follow, with a condition: more iterations pair with padding, not without it.

1. The question: slow convergence, or the destination itself?

Phase A established the artifact and the instruments; Phase B showed that scheduling the regularization by granularity makes the streaks worse, not better. That negative result pushed attention to a fact both testbeds kept showing: the low-frequency-in-z streak severity does not just persist through the late iterations — it grows, slowly and steadily, while every global convergence measure (residual, objective) improves. On the hardened synthetic testbed (the laterally truncated slab with the larger cone angle, Appendix B of the Phase A page), the severity climbs monotonically from around iteration 2 with no sign of turning over.

Two very different explanations fit that observation, and they demand opposite remedies:

  1. Drift. The iteration dynamics keep injecting partition-shaped error into a subspace with almost no restoring force, so error accumulates orthogonally to the real descent. If so, shaping the updates (for example, damping the smooth-in-z component of each voxel-cylinder's update) could suppress the accumulation.
  2. Descent. The objective, given laterally truncated data at aggressive regularization settings, genuinely prefers a streaked solution — the run is converging correctly to a minimizer that contains the artifact. If so, no amount of update shaping can remove the persistent field; only changing the objective or the model (padding, weights, prior structure) can.

Why it matters beyond remedies: the release plan contemplates raising the default iteration count from 15 to 25–50. Under explanation (2), more iterations mean more streak, not less — the artifact deepens as the run converges.

2. The experiment: two starts, one convex objective

The objective is convex (p = 2, q = 1.2), so every start converges to the same limit point. That turns the initialization into a probe: run the same reconstruction from two extreme starting points and watch which way each moves.

  • Production start: the standard initialization path — a direct (FDK) reconstruction, scaled by the optimal factor the code applies internally. This is the configuration every earlier run used.
  • Truth start: the ground truth phantom itself (cropped to the reconstruction cylinder), given the same optimal scaling the FDK path receives: α = argmina ‖y − a·Ax0‖2W, applied before the run (an explicitly supplied start deliberately skips the internal scaling).

Both starts ran for 60 iterations — four times the practical budget — with two partition seeds each, identical seed-for-seed so any difference attributes to the start alone. Every iteration is scored against the ground truth: the axial power spectrum P(fz) of the in-plane high-passed error, its low band Slow, the objective at the target regularization, the data residual, and the two-seed difference within each start (hardened geometry: truncated slab + 11.3° cone, ball layer at 0.35 of the height, default damping, sharpness 1.5 / snr_db 35).

The logic: if the truth-started run stays near the truth while the production run's streak field keeps growing, the field is drift. If the truth-started run develops the same field the production run has — moving away from the truth while its objective falls — the field belongs to the minimizer.

One number from the setup is telling on its own. The optimal scalar for the truth start came out α = 1.267: the best data fit obtainable by scaling the true object inflates it by 27%. The out-of-view part of the slab contributes attenuation that the in-view voxels must explain, and brightening everything is the first, crudest way the model can do that.

Four observations pin the verdict:

  1. The merge. Severity, its z-constant core, the residual, and the objective all become start-independent within a handful of iterations, and the final volumes differ by 2% rms — residual start memory, still shrinking. With a convex objective this is exactly what "one minimizer" looks like.
  2. Clean descent. The objective decreases monotonically in every run and agrees across starts at the end to five decimals. Nothing is oscillating, stalling, or diverging; the artifact grows along the descent.
  3. The walk away from truth. Started at the (scaled) truth, the interior error vs the ground truth rises from 0.39 to 0.57 while the data residual falls from 0.208 to 0.089. The streaked solution fits the truncated data 2.3× better than the truth does — the objective is being improved by the artifact.
  4. The transient dies; the field remains. Within each start, the two-seed (partition-seed- dependent) component decays from ~10−4 to ~5×10−7 over 60 iterations. What persists is seed-independent: the solution's own content. At the practical budget (iteration 14) the transient is still two decades above its floor, though — seed effects are far from gone at realistic iteration counts.

4. Where the error lives: an interior version of the flash

Why would the minimizer contain streaks at all? The working mechanism (proposed by Greg on seeing these results, and consistent with every measurement so far): the streak field is the interior counterpart of the truncation flash. The scan's rays pass through slab material outside the field of view; the measured attenuation must be explained by voxels inside it. The familiar symptom is the bright boundary ring — unexplained mass piled at the support edge. But the prior controls where such deposits can sit cheaply: a strong prior suppresses pixel-to-pixel variation and squeezes the deposit onto the ring; at aggressive settings (weak in-plane restoring force), depositing it throughout the interior as fine-scale texture costs little — and fine-scale, column-coherent texture is exactly what the streak metric measures.

The converged error field, and the deposited-mass ledger

How to read it: error of the converged production run against the ground truth, on a ±0.03 window (≈ 3× the slab's value; the boundary flash saturates at this window; the never-updated corners outside the reconstruction cylinder are blanked). Left: axial mid-slice — fine-grained, spatially disorganized texture fills the interior, with the saturated flash ring at the support boundary. Middle: the (x,z) mid-plane — the same texture organized as vertical striping across the slab band (the artifact's defining z-coherence), with the strongest deposits at the slab's top and bottom faces. Right: the mass ledger: integrated over the reconstruction, the error adds +32% of the ground truth's total mass — +17% packed into the outer 5% ring at roughly slab density (the classic flash), and +15% spread through the interior at about a tenth of slab density. The interior deposit is fine-scale dominated: at the mid slice, the pixel-scale component carries about twice the power of the smooth component.

Three details tie the ledger to the mechanism. First, the deposit is positive on net in both zones — this is added attenuation, not symmetric noise. Second, the interior deposit concentrates at slab heights in z (its per-slice mean peaks near the slab band), inheriting the missing mass's own z-envelope — a natural source for the field's z-organization. Third, the 27% over-brightening that the optimal scalar applies to the true object is the same phenomenon seen from the other end: the model wants more mass than the in-view truth contains.

Scope. These are synthetic-testbed results. On the real scan the same fingerprints appear — the severity's late growth, its z-organization, the flash ring, the settings dependence — but the closing experiments (below) are what check the transfer. One reinterpretation already firms up: the real-scan severity curves on the Phase A page are measured against a conservative-settings reference (sharpness 0 / snr_db 30), not against truth. Their late rise — error growing "away from the reference" — is precisely what a run converging to the aggressive-settings minimizer should do relative to a low-regularization solution, matching the truth-start curve here with the conservative solution standing in for the truth.

5. Immediate implications

  • The remedy map splits by component. The persistent, seed-independent field is the minimizer's content: no update shaping, damping, or schedule can remove it — the fix must change what the objective asks for. Candidates, in order of current promise: lateral support padding (give the mass a legitimate home; already the flash-remediation recommendation for this scan class), boundary/weight handling, prior structure. The seed-dependent transient is real at practical budgets (still two decades above floor at iteration 14) and remains a legitimate target for update-side tools — per-cylinder smooth-in-z damping of the updates is the natural candidate, now aimed at the right component.
  • Iteration-count policy inherits a warning. On this testbed, severity grows toward its plateau as the run converges — raising the default iteration count buys convergence into the artifact. Whether the real scan shares this, and how steeply, is measured in §6d: it does, and steeply.
  • The metric's meaning sharpens. Severity against a converged reference mixes "how far from converged" with "how streaked is the destination." With the destination itself now carrying the artifact, reference-free rulers (the two-seed difference) and truth-based rulers (synthetic) divide the labor cleanly.

6. The follow-up wave: the mechanism tested, the remedies measured

Three experiments ran immediately on these results: a sharpness sweep of the deposit ledger (the mechanism's direct test), lateral padding on both testbeds (the causal test, and the leading remedy), and the real-scan long pair (the transfer test). All three completed; each gets a verdict below.

{{FIG:sweep_partition}}

Left: the total deposit (gray) is flat — 31.5% to 32.6% across a sweep that moves the streak severity 16× — and the ring/interior split (orange/blue) moves only mildly. Right: what moves is the expression scale: the fine-scale fraction of the interior deposit climbs from 0.52 to 0.95 (green, left axis), and the streak severity (red dashed, log right axis) tracks it.

The hypothesis behind this sweep — the streaks as an interior version of the flash — made three predictions, and the sweep grades each one. Conservation: confirmed. The total deposit is set by the data mismatch, and the prior cannot change it. The ring/interior split: inverted. The prediction was that a stronger prior squeezes the deposit onto the ring; measured, the ring share rises with sharpness and the interior share falls. A strong prior does not push the deposit outward — it spreads it inward as a smooth interior bias (the "whole-interior bias" the flash pages characterized), while a weak prior lets the deposit concentrate: a sharper ring spike and pixel-scale interior texture. Texture: confirmed, and it is the part that matters. Severity tracks the fine-scale fraction, not the interior mass share. The refined mechanism in one line: the data fixes how much mass must be deposited; the prior sets the spatial scale at which it is expressed; and the fine-scale, z-organized expression is the streak artifact.

6b. Padding cures the testbed — the causal chain closes

Reconstructing the hardened case on a laterally enlarged grid (1.5×, the object fully inside the support) collapses everything at once: the deposit falls from +32% to +6% of true mass; the streak severity vs truth lands at 4.0×10−6 at iteration 59 — 156× below the unpadded solution — and the z-coherence ratio drops from ~88 to 1.2, i.e. the artifact's defining axial organization is simply gone. The severity now decreases with iteration, and the error against truth falls to 0.195 (from 0.57 unpadded) and keeps improving. Give the out-of-view mass a legitimate home and the interior flash — and with it the streaks — does not form. (A detail worth remembering: the padded solution slightly under-fills the original region, −8% of true mass — the exterior absorbs a little more than its true share.)

6c. Padding on the real scan: the ring goes; the streaks mostly stay

Lateral padding 1.5× vs the baseline on the real scan
{{FIG:pad_vs_unpad_bga}}

How to read it: iteration-16 finals, same seed, same settings; left = unpadded baseline, middle = the padded run's central crop, right = their difference (dominated by a half-voxel grid-registration offset at the metal balls — 1.5× of 510 gives an odd padding delta — so read the side-by-side panels, not the difference). What it shows: the padded run has no boundary flash arcs (bottom row: the bright partial ring of the baseline is gone) and reveals real board structure beyond the old field of view (right edge of the middle panels) — exactly the flash-remediation result. But the fine vertical striping around the ball grid persists visibly, and the two-seed streak severity at iteration 14 drops only 23% (0.00116 vs the baseline's 0.00151) — far from the ≥2× a remedy would need.

The two-sided outcome is informative rather than disappointing: on the synthetic, lateral truncation is the only pathology, and removing it removes everything. On the real scan it is one contributor among several — the metal ball grid (beam hardening, detector effects) and possible axial truncation are all still present — so absorbing the lateral flash removes the ring but only a fraction of the streak field at the practical budget. The discriminating follow-up — the padded long pair, 60 iterations at an even-delta scale (1.502, so the crop is voxel-exact against the reference and the unpadded runs) — sharpens this considerably: padding eliminates the late-time growth entirely. The unpadded severity-vs-reference curve grows ×10.5 from its minimum; the padded curve is flat to ±5% from iteration ~14 on (its z-constant component likewise: unpadded ×12, padded flat). And the seed-dependent transient decays faster padded — 3× lower by iteration 59 (5.5×10−5 vs 1.7×10−4) — consistent with the flash no longer feeding partition-shaped kicks. The flat padded curve sits at a higher level than the unpadded minimum, but that level is measured against the unpadded conservative reference, which contains its own ring and deposit — the ruler cannot separate "the padded solution's residual streaks" from "the reference's own artifact content" (a padded conservative reference would split them, if ever needed). The growth verdict is ruler-robust either way: on the real scan, the converging-into-the-artifact dynamic is lateral-truncation-driven, and padding removes it.

One honest caveat on the ruler: on the real scan there is no ground truth, and the distance-from-the-conservative-solution band also contains legitimate sharp-vs-smooth solution differences (genuinely sharper edges are in-plane-fine and z-coherent too), so the ×10 growth number overstates pure artifact growth by some amount. The iteration-count decision now has real-data numbers — and a condition. On the default (unpadded) configuration, running past the practical budget moves the reconstruction monotonically deeper into the aggressive-settings solution: the streak-band distance from the conservative solution grows 2.3× by iteration 24 and ≈10× by iteration 59, while the data residual improves only from 0.1200 to 0.1133 — raising default max_iterations from 15 to 25–50 at these settings buys little data fit and a steadily stronger expression of the deposit field. With lateral padding the trade-off inverts: the severity curve is flat while the seed-dependent transient keeps dying (20× further from iteration 14 to 59), so extra iterations are purely beneficial. Padding and a higher iteration count belong together; a higher count without padding makes truncated scans worse.

Truncation alone, in a geometry where the projector couples nothing across z, produces the z-organized streak deposit at 134× the contained level — growing through the run, with the interior error vs truth (0.575) essentially equal to the cone case's, and the residual objective 240× the contained case's (the unexplainable-mass signature). The mechanism needs no cone: it is in-plane deposit physics plus the prior's z-coupling. The shipped damping being cone-specific is consistent with where the transient actually lives.

6f. Where this leaves the remedy map

  • Lateral padding is the remedy for the growth. It removes the flash ring, the late-iteration convergence-into-the-artifact dynamic (×10.5 → flat on the real scan), and speeds the transient's decay — and it unlocks higher iteration counts. What it does not do is reach the ≥2× two-seed bar at the 15-iteration budget (−23–25% across the two padded runs), because at short budgets the transient is partition dynamics, only weakly flash-fed.
  • The settings themselves are the other objective-side lever. Severity tracks the expression scale, so backing sharpness off (where a use case allows it) directly de-fines the deposit; the artifact studied here is, mechanically, the price of aggressive settings under truncation — in any geometry (§6e).
  • Update-side tools remain relevant only for the seed-dependent transient at practical budgets (still two decades above its floor at iteration 14 on the real scan). The transient is cone-fed (33× weaker in parallel), which both explains the shipped damping's cone scope and keeps the per-cylinder smooth-in-z damping idea coherently aimed: same component, transpose direction. It stays parked pending a decision on whether the padded + more-iterations pairing already covers the practical need.

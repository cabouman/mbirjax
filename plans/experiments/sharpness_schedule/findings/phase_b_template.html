Does the per-granularity schedule remove the streaks? The Phase B evaluation

The sharpness/snr_db schedule study, Phase B (2026-07-25/26), branch sharpness_schedule. Plan of record: phase_b_plan.md (pre-registered criteria) · Phase A background: findings.html (the artifact, the metrics, and their validation) · scripts: plans/experiments/sharpness_schedule/schedule/.

TL;DR

Phase B asked: does the per-granularity schedule — conservative regularization on the coarse early subsets, target values on the fine ones — reduce the partition-driven streak severity without harming convergence? The pre-registered answer is no.

  • All six variants failed the streak criterion — in the harmful direction. At the gate iteration the two-seed Slow is 4–22% worse than baseline (the criterion required ≤50% of baseline), and the injection peaks are 11–57% worse — real effects against a measured 1% pair noise, and visible from iteration 0 onward.
  • The no-harm criterion passed everywhere (target-objective gaps +0.02–0.04% against a 0.5% allowance): the schedules cost almost nothing — they simply make the artifact worse rather than better.
  • The mechanism reading is clean: prior-side schedules are consistently worse than data-side schedules at matched depth — the pre-registered signature of freshly injected differences being pushed into the prior's saturated regime during formation.

Decision (per the pre-registered rules): no winner; the deeper-schedule extension was not run (it was reserved for a flat response, and the measured response is clearly directional); no full-resolution confirmation; the search stopped for discussion. §6 lays out what the result redirects.

1. What was tested, and how it is judged

Phase A established that the streak artifact is planted by the coarse early subsets and measured it with the two-seed Slow (the low-axial-frequency energy of the fine-scale error in the difference of two reconstructions differing only in their partition seed — definitions and validation on the Phase A page). Phase B evaluates the remedy: one (σx, σy) pair per granularity — the coarse early iterations run at conservative regularization, the fine iterations at the target values, so the converged answer is untouched (the objective is convex) and only the path changes.

A schedule variant is a family and a depth. The depth b is the amount of regularization added per granularity level, measured in decibels of the data-to-prior balance σx²/σy² (sharpness +1 ≈ +6.02 dB); the level d counts granularities below the finest (d = 3, 2, 1 for the default sequence's 4-, 16- and 64-subset iterations). Each family was run at two depths, b = 2 and b = 4 dB per level. A variant is named by its family letter and depth — D2, D4, S2, S4, J2, J4 — six variants in all, judged against the unscheduled baseline.

The judgment is pre-registered (phase_b_plan.md), with thresholds frozen after a noise-calibration step (B0) on the existing Phase A runs — the seed-pair spread of the final Slow is 1% (downsampled) and 4% (full resolution), and the es_rmse seed spread is ≤1.5×10−4, so both margins below dwarf the measurement noise:

  • C1 (streak benefit): two-seed Slow at iteration 14 ≤ ½ of baseline on BOTH scales, every seed pair; and the peak over the snapshot grid ≤ 0.7× baseline's (reduce injection, not merely speed the heal).
  • C2 (no harm): the objective evaluated at the TARGET parameters within +0.5% of baseline at iteration 14 (downsampled arbiter; at full resolution, es_rmse — the root-mean-square sinogram residual, independent of the sigma parameters — and the data term), with a bounded fallback at iteration 16 against baseline's iteration 14. All runs are 17 iterations (numbered 0–16) with early stopping disabled.
  • C3 (no harm where nothing is wrong): the same ≤+0.5% on the synthetic control, which has no artifact.

Metrics are not the whole judgment: every run keeps permanent image panels (final reconstruction + error, and per-iteration error views), and the selection below awaits a visual review of the winner's panels.

2. The search: six variants on the downsampled scan

The search ran the six variants and the baseline on the downsampled BGA scan — seeds {1, 2}, 17 iterations, snapshots on the same iteration grid as Phase A ({0–5, 9, 14, 16}) — through the same segmented driver as every Phase A run, after the driver's equivalence gate passed on the GPU, including a new identity check that a schedule with all offsets zero reproduces the unscheduled baseline. Two further identity checks confirmed the design intent: the automatically derived target (σx, σy) values are bitwise-identical across all runs, and every scheduled run uses exactly those values on its finest-granularity iterations. Before interpreting, one rival reading must be closed: could the elevated Slow at a fixed iteration simply be a convergence-lag artifact — any slower variant scoring worse at equal iteration count? The recorded diagnostics refute this decisively. On both global progress rulers, the variants are barely behind at all: their objective and es_rmse gaps (+0.02–0.04%) correspond to 0.04–0.09 iterations of baseline progress. On the severity trajectory, their elevation corresponds to 0.4–1.8 iterations — a 5–40× mismatch. Sharper still: at iteration 0 the D variants are actually ahead of baseline on the residual (es_rmse −0.15% and −0.27%) while +37% and +20% on severity. The excess is specific to the partition-driven artifact, not a by-product of slower convergence.

The most informative detail is then where the harm appears: immediately. At iteration 0 the variants already sit 20–76% above baseline (D4 +20%, D2 +37%, S4 +76%), before any fine iteration has run. That refutes the deferral story this study half-expected (injection pushed into the fine phase). What remains is a family of closely related readings that the present data do not separate: the coarse phase's strong steps erase seed-dependent error by homogenizing the residual across subsets, so throttling them slows the erasure; and each conservative early subset leaves a larger residual for the later coarse subsets, which then plant more (the Phase A footprint mechanism run in reverse). On either reading, the marginal effect of stronger coarse steps is net erasure at this scale — and one caveat is recorded against both: the harm is not monotone in depth in the D family (§5), so the story is incomplete.

One diagnostic did move as the hypothesis predicted — with a catch. The footprint enrichment E(0) softened in every variant (1.41 → 1.33–1.37): the first-updated subset's share of the streak energy fell. But the map's total energy rose by a similar factor, so the absolute streak energy on the first-updated subset's cylinders was essentially unchanged (−3% to +1%): the schedule redistributed the association without reducing the injection it was aimed at.

3. Why there is no full-resolution confirmation

The plan's decision rules promote a variant to full-resolution confirmation only after it passes C1 + C2 on the downsampled search. No variant did, so the confirmation was not run — a decision made by rule, not by judgment, and worth stating precisely: the full-resolution question is closed by pre-registration, not by direct measurement. The downsampled result still argues a full-resolution benefit is unlikely, on observational grounds: every variant's injection peak worsened (+11–57%), and Phase A measured full resolution as the regime where peaks are recovered from far more slowly (a factor 3.6 from the peak, against 9 downsampled) — so elevated peaks should persist harder there, not resolve. A deliberate exploratory run of the least-harmful variant at full resolution remains an option for discussion, clearly labeled as outside the pre-registered protocol.

4. The guards that did not fire

The remaining protocol elements were conditioned on outcomes that did not occur, and correctly never ran: the third seeds (reserved for near-threshold calls — nothing was near threshold; the failures exceed the 1% pair noise by factors of 4–22), the 40-iteration merge demonstration and the synthetic no-harm runs (C3, both conditioned on a winner), and the b = 8 depth extension (reserved for a flat C1 response; the response was directional). The registered run-health diagnostics were recorded and are clean: no line-search clips at either bound, and the preconditioner prior-share behaved as designed (rising with schedule depth in the coarse phase — used in §5). The B0 noise calibration stands as this page's measurement-noise reference: final-Slow pair spread 1% (downsampled) and 4% (full resolution); es_rmse seed spread ≤1.5×10−4.

5. The mechanism reading: which knob mattered?

The families were built so that, at equal depth b, the D and S coarse-phase sigma pairs differ by an exact common multiplier — and every update term except the prior's saturation-threshold factors is invariant under that scaling. Any systematic D–S gap is therefore a threshold effect. (The plan registered readings for both possible directions; the direction below is the one in which the prior-side family performs worse.) The data give a clear sign: S is consistently worse than D at equal depth (at iteration 14, 0.00176 vs 0.00168 at b = 2 and 0.00184 vs 0.00158 at b = 4; at iteration 0 the b = 4 gap is 0.0128 vs 0.0087). Per the registered interpretation, this is the signature of saturation during formation: the S-family's lowered early edge threshold T·σx — the difference scale beyond which the qGGMRF prior treats a voxel-neighbor difference as an edge and its restoring force saturates — pushes freshly injected differences into that saturated regime, where the prior is weakest. The ramp freezes the very structure it was meant to prevent.

Two quantitative checks corroborate the threshold reading. The S–D gap grows with depth, as a threshold effect should when the threshold falls further (the S/D severity ratio at iteration 14 rises from 1.05 at b = 2 to 1.17 at b = 4). And the J family, which carries half of each knob by construction, lands almost exactly halfway between D and S on a log scale (interpolation fraction 0.54 at both depths) — the response is smooth in the designed coordinates, not idiosyncratic per family.

Two observations recorded without over-interpretation. The D family is non-monotonic in depth: D4 is less harmful than D2 at every iteration, including iteration 0 (+20% vs +37%) — a formation-phase property that the simple slowed-erasure story does not predict; the natural variable for a future resolution is the recorded preconditioner prior-share, which shifts regime across depth (at iteration 0: baseline 0.35, D2 0.69, D4 0.90 — prior-dominated). And the E(0) result (§2) is a redistribution, not a reduction: the first-updated subset's share fell while its absolute streak energy stayed flat — the first-subset bias is real but is not the controlling term in total severity at this scale.

6. Where this leaves the remedy question

The per-granularity regularization schedule, as constructed, is not the remedy: on the downsampled scan it fails its primary criterion in the harmful direction at every family and depth, while costing essentially nothing in convergence. This is a clean, pre-registered negative, and it earns two redirections:

  • The target moves from “early oversteps” to “subset homogenization”. The artifact's severity tracks how quickly the iteration erases subset-patterned error, and the marginal effect of stronger coarse steps is net erasure at this scale (§2 — the coarse phase still plants the artifact; weakening it just removes more erasing than planting). Remedies that act on the seed-dependent field directly — the per-slice damping family already in the library, which demonstrably suppresses the z-varying component on the real scan, and mode-selective extensions of it — look better aimed than schedules that throttle the early iterations.
  • The saturation finding gives a second lever: the S-family's failure mode says the prior's threshold should stay HIGH while streaks form — any future schedule attempt should not lower T·σx early. On the opposite direction: an aggressive-early schedule was not tested (only conservative decrements were), so the data neither support nor rule it out; the baseline was best among everything tested. Caution rather than data argues against it — Phase A showed globally aggressive settings worsen severity, and the D-family's depth non-monotonicity warns against extrapolating smooth trends through b = 0.

Discussion items for the technical lead: whether to run a deliberate exploratory full-resolution check of D4 (outside the protocol, cheaply bounding the “maybe it helps where the artifact persists” question); and whether Phase C pivots to the damping-family direction. One caution on test vehicles for that pivot: the hardened synthetic (Phase A page, Appendix B) is a fast testbed for truncation-driven streaking, but it is damping-insensitive as built — a damping-family remedy iterated there would read as a null regardless of its real-scan value — so damping-family work should be evaluated on the downsampled real scan (or the testbed first extended until it shows real-scan-like damping sensitivity). A visual pass over the per-run image panels (kept for every run; locations in Provenance) supports both questions.
